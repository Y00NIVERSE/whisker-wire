## Comprehension check

**1. What does Single source mean on a story?** Only one news outlet or data source reported this story. It hasn't been verified or covered elsewhere yet. Usually means it's newer, less confirmed, or sometimes just unknown sources. Makes me skeptical.

**2. What does Under the radar mean?** The story isn't getting a lot of attention or coverage yet. Investors might not have noticed it. Could be an opportunity if it's important, but feels risky if I'm the only one paying attention.

**3. What do the big number and the URGENT label on a story tell you?** The big number (like "8") is the importance ranking from 1-100. URGENT means it's in the top urgent stories right now and should matter to my money. It's the ranking system at work.

**4. What does Corroborated mean?** Multiple sources reported the same story. More trustworthy because it's been covered by multiple outlets. The opposite of single source.

**5. What does the Value Radar score mean, and is a high score a buy signal?** The score (0-100) measures how discounted/cheap a stock is relative to its fundamentals (earnings, book value, 52-week high, analyst view). A high score like 76 means it's cheap by multiple measures. But high score is NOT automatically a buy signal - you still have to check the "How it could be a trap" section to see if the discount is a value or a value trap. The traps section showed PLNT is leveraged and in a downtrend, so despite 76/100, I wouldn't buy it yet.

**6. What is Tick's job, and how much should you trust her highlights?** Tick the cat highlights the important parts of articles and provides context about why they matter. She pulls out key numbers, red flags, and positive signals. I'd trust her highlights for identifying what's important and seeing patterns, but I'd do my own research on the actual numbers and reasoning. She's a guide, not a final verdict.

**7. In one sentence: what is this site's promise, and do you believe it?** The site promises to rank financial news by importance, explain why it matters, and save you from bad decisions - it's news for thoughtful investing, not gambling. I believe it can help, but it requires you to do the final thinking yourself.

## What could be different

**Ideas for improvements:**

1. **Jargon glossary:** Every investing site assumes you know what "guidance," "Form 4," "forward P/E," and "book value" mean. Maya doesn't. Add a sidebar glossary or inline tooltips so she doesn't feel dumb.

2. **Simpler watchlist flow:** The watchlist add felt like it failed. Make it obvious that it worked (show the ticker in a list immediately, show a confirmation message, let her click the ticker to filter the wire to just that stock).

3. **Why this news, not that news:** The wire ranks stories but doesn't always explain *why* story #1 matters more than story #10. Add a note like "This ranks higher because: insiders buying (positive signal) + analyst upgrade (confirmation)."

4. **Easier Filings access:** Don't gatekeep insider trades behind a technical file setup. Just ask for an email and send a daily digest, or show a web form.

5. **Mobile-first defaults:** The site works on mobile but feels designed for desktop. On mobile, Maya would want one story at a time, maybe a swipe-through interface, not scrolling through a dense list.

6. **Tick integration with ticker search:** When Maya clicks into a stock, Tick could immediately analyze the most recent 3-5 articles about that company (like, "What's happening with PLNT right now?") instead of waiting for her to manually paste text.

**What competitors do better:**

- **Reddit r/investing:** Free, fast, community explains the why, but sometimes full of bad advice
- **Robinhood alerts:** Simple, immediate, but only alerts about price movement, not fundamentals
- **YouTube channels (e.g., Graham Stephan, Financial Education):** Deep context and explanation, but takes 20 minutes per video
- **Seeking Alpha Premium:** More detailed analysis but paywall, sometimes feels salesy
- **MarketWatch:** Free news but not ranked by importance, no synthesis

**What I use today:**
- Robinhood (for buying/selling, basic news alerts)
- Reddit r/investing (for understanding what other people think)
- YouTube explainers (when I hit a concept I don't understand)
- MarketWatch (random deep-dive when I want context on a specific stock)

The gap Whisker Wire is filling: news that's been ranked for relevance AND explained with context, without the hype. That's valuable. But it's not quite there yet because of the jargon and the unclear feature interactions.
